Reading the table

  • "Not stated" means the app's own page we read doesn't say, not that the app can't do it.
  • Accounts and sign-in. Final Draft's product page says a license is used to sign in, with an internet connection needed for the first sign-in, on up to two computers at once [1]. Cold Open has no account and no sign-in, and runs on as many computers as you like.
  • Cold Open is an alpha. The others are mature products with features Cold Open doesn't have yet, such as a spellchecker, index cards and, for some, real-time co-writing. Known limitations lists what's missing.

Where Cold Open is different

  • Free and open, for good: no purchase, subscription or account, and source code anyone can read and build.
  • A crash journal: every keystroke is saved before it shows on screen, so a crash never costs what you typed.
  • .fdx byte for byte: saving an .fdx file back writes it as it came, keeping what Cold Open doesn't use.
  • The same pages everywhere: bundled fonts and its own layout, so a script is the same length on Windows, macOS and Linux.
